Cast Iron Stove Controller

We have a cast iron stove from Vermont Castings. It runs on natural gas and came with electronics and a WiFi-based remote control that acts as a thermostat and allows control of the flame level, fan, and more.

The entire design was flawed from the start. The remote contained the thermometer and controlled the flame on the stove — not unusual for the sunporch to be blazing hot or freezing cold. The remote stopped functioning correctly and Honeywell discontinued it, so a replacement was not available. Replacing it meant replacing the wireless controller in the stove as well as all of the control valves: $700.

My initial plan was to get a flipper 0 and use that to figure out what commands were being sent on which frequency. This I realized when I really wanted was the equivalent of a mercury switch thermostat.

I got out a multimeter and started tracing wires. I eventually found two wires that gave me the limited control I needed. I opened my drawer of microcontrollers and parts and assembled an Arduino-based solution. As with everything that actually works, it is really simple:

  • Arduino Nano
  • Temperature sensor
  • High-voltage relay (overkill in retrospect)
  • USB power supply
  • LED indicators to show relay state
  • Hot glue to hold it all together

I plumbed it into the wires I identified and it works like a charm. It is so reliable I forgot it exists. I put this in the category of things I wish I had done ten years ago.
